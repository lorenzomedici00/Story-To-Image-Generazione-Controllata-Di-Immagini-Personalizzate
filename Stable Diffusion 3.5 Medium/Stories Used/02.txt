A three-year-old girl, the daughter of a destitute woodcutter, was taken from her impoverished home by the Virgin Mary, who descended from the heavens wearing a crown of shining stars. The Virgin brought the child to paradise, where she was raised in splendor, eating sugar-cakes, drinking sweet milk, and playing with the little angels.

When the girl reached fourteen, the Virgin Mary entrusted her with the thirteen keys of heaven, announcing she would be taking a long journey. The girl was permitted to open twelve doors to behold the glory within but was strictly forbidden to unlock the thirteenth door, the key to which was a small, unassuming one.

Once alone, the girl's curiosity became an unbearable torment. Despite the angels' warnings that it would be a sin and cause unhappiness, she crept up to the forbidden door. She placed the small key in the lock and turned it, revealing the Holy Trinity in fire and splendor.

The girl touched the light in amazement, and her finger was immediately stained pure gold. Overcome by terror, she slammed the door shut, but the gold mark remained, and her heart beat violently. When the Virgin Mary returned, the girl lied three times about opening the door, and the golden finger exposed her sin and disobedience.

Cast down to Earth and struck mute in a desolate wilderness, she lived years in misery within a hollow tree, her long hair growing to cover her like a mantle. She was eventually found by a king and became his queen, but her silence and repeated denial of her sin led the Virgin Mary to take away her three newborn children. Only when the queen confessed her sin aloud while tied to a burning stake did the flames extinguish, and the Virgin Mary returned her children and her voice, granting forgiveness.
