A king and queen, already blessed with twelve sons, faced a tragic decree from the king: if their thirteenth child was a girl, the twelve boys must die to ensure the daughter's inheritance. The king had twelve coffins prepared and gave the key to the queen. Heartbroken, the queen confessed the plot to her youngest son, Benjamin, who advised his brothers to flee into the forest.

The queen promised to raise a flag on the tower: white if she bore a son, red if she bore a daughter. When Benjamin's turn came to keep watch, he saw the blood-red flag hoisted. The twelve brothers, enraged that they were condemned for a girl, swore an oath to kill any maiden they encountered. They settled in a dark, bewitched hut in the forest, where Benjamin, as the youngest, was made to stay home and keep house while the others hunted.

Ten years later, their little sister—a girl of great beauty with a golden star on her forehead—discovered her brothers' shirts and the twelve empty coffins. Learning the truth, she took the shirts and set out into the forest to find them. She eventually reached the hut, where Benjamin, recognizing her, revealed himself and the boys' fatal oath.

The sister declared that she would gladly die to save them, but Benjamin hid her and convinced his brothers to spare the first maiden they met. Overjoyed to find their sister, they lived in harmony. However, the girl unknowingly plucked twelve white lily flowers from the garden—which were her brothers—and they instantly transformed into twelve ravens, disappearing forever, and the hut vanished.

An old woman revealed the only way to save her brothers: the maiden must remain mute for seven years and never laugh. She accepted the ordeal. A hunting king found her, made her his silent queen, and loved her dearly. When the seven years were about to expire, the king, urged by his wicked mother, condemned the queen to be burned. Just as the fire was lit, the twelve ravens descended, returned to their human forms, extinguished the flames, and the rescued sister finally spoke, telling her story to the joyful king.
