A peasant, having sold his cow for seven talers at the fair, was walking home when he passed a pond. The frogs within began to croak, “Aik, aik, aik, aik,” a sound the foolish man mistook for them arguing that he had received eight talers, not seven.

Infuriated by the "stupid animals," the peasant insisted on counting his seven talers out loud for them. When the frogs persisted with their "Aik, aik, aik, aik", he grew so angry that he threw all his money into the water, challenging them to count it themselves. He stood waiting in vain until evening, when he was forced to go home empty-handed.

Later, the peasant took his second slaughtered cow's meat to town. Outside the gate, he encountered a pack of dogs led by a large greyhound that barked "Wow, wow, wow." The peasant convinced himself the dog was agreeing to be a surety for the others, leaving the entire load of meat with the canine pack on the promise of payment in three days.

When the promised payment never arrived, the peasant went to the butcher, who, believing the story to be a joke, drove him away with a broomstick. Seeking justice, the peasant went before the king and related his tale of the frogs, the dogs, and the butcher's stick, a lamentation so absurd that it caused the king's daughter to laugh heartily for the very first time.

The king immediately offered his daughter as a wife—the reward he had long promised—but the peasant refused her. Instead, he was offered five hundred talers. The peasant, in his greed and folly, immediately promised away the entire sum to a soldier and a Jew, who were subsequently paid in well-counted blows for their claim. In the end, the peasant's shrewd final trick of wearing the Jew's coat secured him a handsome reward from the king and the last laugh.
