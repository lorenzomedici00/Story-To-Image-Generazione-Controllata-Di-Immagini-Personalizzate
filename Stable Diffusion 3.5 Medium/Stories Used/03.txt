A younger son, branded as stupid and unable to grasp anything, stood apart from his older, sensible brother. While others would speak of "shuddering" with fear when hearing chilling tales or walking through the churchyard, the boy did not understand the feeling; he concluded that shuddering must be an art he had not yet learned, and he desired to master it.

The boy was sent to the sexton for instruction, who promised to "polish him" and teach him to shudder. The sexton's first lesson involved sending the boy to the church tower at midnight to ring the bell, while he secretly waited on the stairs, disguised in a white sheet to appear as a ghost and frighten the youth.

Upon reaching the bell-rope, the boy saw the motionless white figure and, without any trace of fear, issued three warnings to speak or leave the premises. When the figure remained silent, the boy, taking him for a scoundrel, swiftly shoved the "ghost" down the stairs, causing the sexton to break his leg, then calmly rang the bell and returned to sleep.

The boy was cast out by his furious father with a mere fifty talers and a command to never reveal his origins. He set off into the world, muttering his singular wish: "If I could but shudder." His journey led him to a haunted castle, where he spent three nights, killing two demonic cats, fighting black dogs, and playing nine-pins with the body parts of dead men, still unable to feel any fear.

After surviving the third night and outwitting an old bearded fiend to claim the castle's treasure, the youth married the king's beautiful daughter, as promised. Yet, he still lamented that he could not shudder. Finally, his wife, taking the advice of her maid, poured a bucket of cold water and gudgeons (small fish) over him while he slept, making him leap up in shock and cry, "Oh, what makes me shudder so! Now I know what it is to shudder!"
