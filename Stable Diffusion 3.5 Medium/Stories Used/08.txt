A little brother and sister, living miserably under the cruel tyranny of their step-mother after their own mother's death, decided to run away and seek fortune in the wide world. They walked all day until, exhausted, they fell asleep in a hollow tree in a vast forest.

The next morning, the brother was overcome with thirst and wanted a drink. However, their step-mother was a wicked witch who had followed them and cursed every stream in the forest. As they approached the first brook, the sister heard it whisper a warning: whoever drinks of it will become a tiger. At the second, the warning was: whoever drinks will become a wolf.

Overcome by his immense thirst, the brother vowed he would drink from the third stream regardless of the risk. The sister heard its warning—whoever drinks will become a roebuck. The boy drank the water immediately and instantly transformed into a gentle young roebuck. The heartbroken sister wept, but quickly tied her golden garter and a rope of rushes around the roebuck’s neck, promising never to leave him, and they settled in an abandoned cottage deep in the woods.

One day, the roebuck heard the sounds of a royal hunt and begged his sister to let him run. She agreed, but made him promise to return at night and use the password: "My little sister, let me in." The king, who was leading the hunt, spotted the beautiful animal with its golden collar and was enchanted. A huntsman, tracking the slightly wounded roebuck, saw him use the password to enter the mysterious cottage.

The king, using the same password, entered the cottage and, finding the beautiful girl, asked her to be his wife. She accepted, provided the little roebuck could come, too. The witch, furious at their happiness, later disguised herself and suffocated the Queen in a bath, replacing her with her own ugly, one-eyed daughter. The true Queen, however, returned as a spirit at midnight for three nights to nurse her baby and stroke her roebuck, uttering the rhyme: "How fares my child, how fares my roe. This time I come, then never more." On the final night, the watching king embraced his wife, instantly restoring her to life, and as the wicked witch was burned, the roebuck returned to his human form forever.
