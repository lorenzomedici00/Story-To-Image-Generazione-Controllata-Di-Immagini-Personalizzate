An old king, nearing his death, summoned his most trusted and lifelong servant, Faithful John. The king, deeply worried about his young son's future, made John promise to act as the prince's foster-father and guide, ensuring he learned everything necessary, even if it cost the servant his life.

The king gave John one specific, final instruction: to show the prince all the palace's treasures and chambers, except the last one in the long gallery. This hidden room contained the portrait of the Princess of the Golden Dwelling, a sight the old king knew would cause his son to fall violently in love and face great danger.

After the king's death, the young prince eventually noticed the one locked door that Faithful John always avoided. Driven by an insatiable desire, the prince insisted on seeing what lay inside, despite John's tearful warnings about the misfortune it would bring. Once the door was opened, the prince saw the magnificent portrait and instantly fainted to the ground in a passionate swoon.

Upon recovering, the young king declared his overwhelming love for the princess and commanded his servant to help him win her. Faithful John devised a plan: he commissioned five tons of gold to be fashioned into exquisite wares, disguised the king and himself as merchants, and sailed to the princess's land to lure her onto their ship with the promise of showing her the unrivaled golden treasures.

As they sailed away, John, sitting at the helm, overheard three ravens foretelling the king's ruin: a lethal chestnut horse, a poisonous bridal garment, and the queen's death by swoon. Three times, John swiftly acted to save his master, resulting in him becoming stone by the third act. The king, not understanding, condemned his faithful servant. John's life was only restored when the repentant king sacrificed his own twin children, a deed of fidelity that John immediately reversed, reuniting the happy family.
