A childless couple lived next to a magnificent garden belonging to a powerful, feared enchantress. The wife, while pregnant, developed an overwhelming craving for the rampion (rapunzel) growing there, to the point of pining away. Fearing for his wife's life, the husband broke into the garden to steal the herb.

Caught by the enchantress, the terrified man was forced to agree to a dreadful condition for his life: he had to surrender the child his wife bore. The enchantress appeared when the baby arrived, named her Rapunzel, and took her away to raise as her own.

When Rapunzel turned twelve, the enchantress locked her in a tall tower without a door or stairs, accessible only by a single window. To enter, the enchantress would call out the magical rhyme: "Rapunzel, Rapunzel, let down your hair," and the girl would unfasten her magnificent, long, golden tresses, allowing the old woman to climb up.

Years later, a king's son rode through the forest, heard Rapunzel's enchanting song, and discovered the enchantress's secret way of entry. He then returned, repeated the rhyme, and ascended to meet Rapunzel. The young lovers immediately plotted her escape by weaving a ladder of silk to be brought over many visits.

Rapunzel's innocent question about why the enchantress was heavier to pull up than the prince betrayed her. Enraged, the enchantress shrieked, cut off Rapunzel's braids, and banished her to a miserable desert. The enchantress then lured the prince up using the severed hair, revealed his loss, and caused him to leap in despair, piercing his eyes. Blind and heartbroken, the prince wandered for years until he finally found Rapunzel and their twin children. Her tears of joy healed his sight, and he led his family back to his kingdom.
