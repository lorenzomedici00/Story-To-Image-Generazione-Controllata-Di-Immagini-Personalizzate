A widower and a widow with a daughter each decided to marry. The widow, attempting to secure a privileged life for her step-daughter, promised the man's child that if the marriage occurred, she would wash in milk and drink wine every morning, while her own daughter would have only water. The man, uncertain about remarrying, proposed a bizarre test: filling his old boot with water to see if it would hold. The water held, the man was convinced, and the wedding was celebrated.

For the first day, the man's daughter received the promised milk and wine. But the step-mother, driven by jealousy, soon altered the pattern: by the third day, the step-daughter was treated with contempt, while her own ugly, repulsive child received the benefits. The step-mother's bitter envy of the step-daughter's growing beauty consumed her, and she sought daily to inflict misery.

In the depths of winter, the cruel step-mother forced the girl to put on a paper frock and go into the frozen forest to search for a basket of strawberries, intending for her to die of cold and hunger. The obedient girl found a small, welcoming house where three little men resided. She courteously shared her single piece of hard bread with them.

In return for her goodness, the little men granted her three gifts: she would grow more beautiful every day; gold pieces would fall from her mouth with every word she spoke; and she would marry a king. She then found the ripe strawberries after sweeping the snow, returned home, and astonished her step-mother and step-sister with a shower of gold coins.

The envious step-sister, trying to replicate the fortune, was rude to the little men and received curses in return: she would grow uglier daily, and toads would leap from her mouth with every word. Later, the step-mother tried to dispose of the beautiful step-daughter by making her cut ice on a frozen river, where the King found her, was smitten, and made her his Queen. When the evil mother and daughter later tried to kill the new Queen, throwing her into a river, the Queen returned for three nights as a duck, but was ultimately restored to life by her husband's sword, and the wicked duo were condemned to die in a barrel of nails.
