A widower, with his daughter, and a widow, with hers, decide to marry. The widower tests the union by filling an old boot with water. To everyone's surprise, the water holds within the worn leather. Convinced by this strange omen, the couple proceeds with their wedding plans. The celebration begins.

Initially, the beautiful step-daughter enjoys promised milk and wine. However, her stepmother's jealousy soon surfaces. The step-mother's own ugly child now receives lavish treats and drinks, while the step-daughter is deliberately given only plain water and contempt. Bitter envy consumes the stepmother, her cruel gaze fixed on the girl.

In deep winter, the step-daughter, clad in a flimsy paper frock, is forced into the frozen forest to find strawberries. Shivering from cold and hunger, she stumbles upon a small, welcoming house. Inside, three kind little men reside. She generously shares her last piece of hard bread with them.

After sweeping snow, the girl miraculously uncovers a basket of ripe strawberries. She returns home, astonishing her stepmother and step-sister. With every word she speaks, gleaming gold pieces shower from her mouth, landing on the floor. Her step-family stares, utterly captivated and filled with avarice.

The envious step-sister speaks, grotesque toads leaping from her mouth. The stepmother then sends the beautiful step-daughter to cut ice on a frozen river. There, a passing King is smitten and marries her. Later, thrown into a river, the Queen becomes a duck. Her husband’s sword restores her; the wicked duo face their grim fate.
