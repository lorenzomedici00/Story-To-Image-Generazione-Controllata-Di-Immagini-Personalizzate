A foolish peasant, having sold his cow, walks past a pond. Frogs on lily pads croak loudly, "Aik, aik, aik!" The peasant believes they argue he received eight talers, not seven. Enraged, he counts his seven silver talers aloud, then angrily throws all his coins into the water, challenging the frogs to count it themselves. He waits in vain until evening.

Later, the peasant carries a basket of slaughtered cow's meat towards town gates. A large greyhound leads a pack of barking dogs, "Wow, wow, wow!" The peasant interprets their barks as a promise. He leaves the entire load of meat with the canine pack, expecting payment in three days.

When payment fails, the peasant confronts a butcher, who, thinking it a joke, drives him away with a broomstick. Seeking justice, the peasant stands before the King on his throne. He recounts the absurd tales of the frogs and dogs. The King's daughter, seated beside him, laughs heartily for the very first time.

The King, delighted, offers his daughter as wife. The peasant, surprisingly, refuses her. Instead, the King presents him with five hundred shining talers. In his boundless greed and folly, the peasant immediately promises the entire sum away to a waiting soldier in uniform and a distinctively dressed Jew.

The soldier and the Jew approach for their promised money. Instead, they receive swift, well-aimed blows. Finally, the peasant, wearing the Jew's coat and hat, stands before the King. With this clever trick, the peasant secures a final, handsome reward of more coins from the King, having the last laugh.
