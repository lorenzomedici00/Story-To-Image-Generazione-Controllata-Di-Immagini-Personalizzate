A foolish peasant, holding seven silver talers from his cow's sale, walks beside a murky pond. Green frogs croak loudly, "Aik, aik, aik," which he misinterprets as an argument about eight talers. Infuriated, he counts his money aloud. When the croaking continues, he throws all seven talers into the water, challenging the frogs. He waits, empty-handed, until evening.

The peasant transports slaughtered cow meat to town. Outside the gate, a large greyhound leads a pack of dogs, barking "Wow, wow, wow." The peasant, deluded, believes the greyhound promises payment. He leaves the entire load of meat with the hungry canine pack, expecting payment in three days.

Three days later, the peasant visits a butcher, demanding payment. The butcher, amused, drives him away with a sturdy broomstick. The peasant then presents his absurd tale to the King: the frogs, the dogs, the butcher's stick. The King's beautiful daughter, hearing the story, laughs heartily for the very first time.

The King offers his daughter's hand in marriage, a long-promised reward for her first laugh. The foolish peasant, however, refuses the princess. Instead, he accepts five hundred shimmering talers. Greedily, he immediately promises the entire sum to a watchful soldier and a waiting Jew, both anticipating their payment.

The peasant pays the soldier and the Jew with well-aimed blows, not money. In a final, cunning move, he cleverly takes the Jew's fine coat. Wearing the stolen coat, the peasant stands before the King once more. Impressed by this shrewdness, the King grants the peasant a substantial, handsome reward, securing his last laugh.
