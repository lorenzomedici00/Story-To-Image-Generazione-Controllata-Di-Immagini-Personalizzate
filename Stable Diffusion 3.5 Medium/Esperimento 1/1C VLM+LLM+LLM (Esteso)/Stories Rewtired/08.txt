A thin young boy and girl, dressed in tattered clothes, silently flee a dark, austere house. They walk through a vast, sun-dappled forest, their figures small against towering trees. Exhausted, they find refuge inside a huge, ancient hollow tree trunk, huddling together for sleep as twilight falls.

Morning light filters through leaves. The boy, parched, urges them towards a stream. The girl stops, hearing whispers from the water: "Whoever drinks will become a tiger." They pass two cursed brooks. At the third, despite her warnings, the boy drinks eagerly, instantly transforming into a gentle roebuck. The girl, weeping, ties a golden garter and rush rope around its neck.

The sister and roebuck reside in a rustic, vine-covered cottage nestled deep in the woods. One day, a royal hunt's distant horns sound. The roebuck runs, a golden garter visible on its neck, drawing the King's attention. A huntsman secretly watches the roebuck approach the cottage and utter a distinct password to enter.

The King, using the password, enters the cottage and proposes to the beautiful girl, who accepts with her roebuck by her side. They marry in a lavish palace. Later, the wicked step-mother, disguised as a kindly old woman, drowns the Queen in a palatial bath. She then replaces the Queen with her own ugly, one-eyed daughter on the throne.

For three nights, a translucent spirit of the Queen secretly hovers over her baby's crib, softly stroking the watchful roebuck. On the third night, the hidden King embraces the spirit, instantly restoring his Queen to life and solid form. Outside, the wicked witch is led to a burning pyre. The roebuck simultaneously transforms back into the boy.
