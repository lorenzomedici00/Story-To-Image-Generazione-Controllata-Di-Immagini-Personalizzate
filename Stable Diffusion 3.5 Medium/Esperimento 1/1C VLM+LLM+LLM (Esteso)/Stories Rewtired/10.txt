A solemn widower, a widow, and her daughter stand together. The widow promises the man's daughter milk and wine, her own daughter water. The man tests an old boot, filling it with water; it holds. Convinced, he marries the widow. A simple wedding ceremony takes place.

Days later, the stepmother pours milk and wine for her own plain daughter, offering only water to the beautiful step-daughter. Her face contorts with bitter jealousy, watching the step-daughter's growing grace. The step-daughter endures contempt daily within the home.

In deep winter, the cruel stepmother sends the step-daughter, clad in a thin paper frock, into the frozen, snow-laden forest. She searches for strawberries. The girl discovers a small, cozy house. Inside, she shares her single piece of hard bread with three friendly little old men.

The little men bestow three gifts: increasing beauty, gold coins falling with every word, and a king's hand. The girl sweeps snow, finds ripe strawberries, and returns home. She speaks, and golden coins shower from her mouth, astounding the stepmother and stepsister.

The envious stepsister is rude, receiving curses: daily ugliness and toads from her mouth. Stepmother sends the beautiful step-daughter to cut river ice. A King finds her, smitten, and weds her. The wicked duo throws the Queen into a river. She returns as a duck, then is restored by the King's sword. The evil stepmother and stepsister are condemned to a barrel of nails.
