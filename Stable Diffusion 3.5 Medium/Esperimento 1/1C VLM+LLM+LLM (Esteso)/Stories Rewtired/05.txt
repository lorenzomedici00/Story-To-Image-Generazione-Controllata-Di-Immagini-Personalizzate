An old king, nearing death, summons Faithful John. The king, deeply worried, makes John promise to guide his young son. He warns John not to show the prince the last room in the long gallery, containing a dangerous portrait of the Princess of the Golden Dwelling.

Years later, the young prince discovers the locked door. Despite John's tearful warnings, the prince insists on opening it. Inside, he sees the magnificent portrait of the Princess. Overwhelmed, the prince instantly faints to the palace floor in a passionate swoon.

Recovering, the young king commands John to help him win the princess. John commissions five tons of gold wares. Disguising themselves as merchants, they sail to the princess's land. They plan to lure her onto their ship with the glittering golden treasures.

John, at the ship's helm, overhears three ravens foretelling doom: a lethal chestnut horse and a poisonous bridal garment. He swiftly averts both dangers, saving the king. With his third act, to save the queen from swooning, John completely turns to stone.

The king, misunderstanding, condemns the stone John. Later, understanding John's fidelity, the king sacrifices his twin children. This act restores John to life. John immediately reverses the sacrifice, bringing the children back. The happy family is reunited, embracing.
