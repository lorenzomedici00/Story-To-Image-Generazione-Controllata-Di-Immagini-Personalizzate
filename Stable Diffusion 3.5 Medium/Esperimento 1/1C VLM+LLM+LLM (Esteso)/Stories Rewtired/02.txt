A three-year-old girl, ragged, stands outside a poor woodcutter's cottage. The Virgin Mary, adorned with a crown of shining stars, descends from celestial clouds. She gently takes the child's hand, lifting her towards a luminous, heavenly realm. Sugar-cakes and sweet milk appear in paradise as little angels play nearby.

Fourteen-year-old girl, now radiant, receives thirteen ornate keys from the Virgin Mary in a celestial chamber. Twelve grand, ornate doors line the hall. The Virgin Mary, departing for a long journey, points to a small, unassuming thirteenth door, strictly forbidding her to unlock it, despite the girl's intense curiosity.

Alone, the girl's curiosity compels her. Ignoring unseen angels' warnings, she stealthily approaches the plain thirteenth door. She inserts the small key. The door swings open to reveal a blinding Holy Trinity in fire and splendor. She reaches out, touching the light, and her finger instantly stains pure gold.

The Virgin Mary returns to paradise. The girl, terrified, tries to hide her golden finger, but the mark is visible. She lies three times about opening the door. With a sorrowful, stern expression, the Virgin Mary casts the disobedient girl down from the celestial heights towards a desolate, barren Earth below.

Mute and alone, the woman lives years inside a hollow tree, her long hair a covering. A king discovers her and makes her queen. Later, tied to a burning stake, she confesses her sin aloud. Flames instantly extinguish. The Virgin Mary reappears, returning her three children and restoring her voice, granting forgiveness.
