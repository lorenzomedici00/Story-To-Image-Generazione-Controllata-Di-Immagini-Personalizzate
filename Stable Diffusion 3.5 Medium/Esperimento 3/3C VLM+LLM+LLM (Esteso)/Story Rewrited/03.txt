A puzzled young boy, deemed "stupid," stands apart from his sensible older brother. Others speak of "shuddering" with fear, but he remains bewildered by the feeling. He observes, concluding it's an art he has yet to master, earnestly desiring to learn this mysterious skill.

The boy is sent to the grim sexton for instruction. At midnight, he climbs the dark, winding stairs of the church tower to ring the bell. Unknown to him, the sexton, draped menacingly in a white sheet, lurks silently on the steps below, intending to appear as a ghost and frighten the youth.

At the bell-rope, the boy calmly faces the motionless white figure. He issues three direct warnings. When it remains silent, he deems it a scoundrel and shoves the "ghost" down the stairs. The sexton tumbles, breaking his leg. Unperturbed, the boy proceeds to ring the bell, then returns to sleep.

Cast out by his furious father with fifty talers, the boy walks a desolate path, muttering his wish to shudder. He reaches a foreboding, haunted castle. Inside, he battles two demonic cats, confronts black dogs, and calmly plays nine-pins using dead men's body parts, still without a trace of fear.

The youth, having claimed the castle treasure, marries the beautiful king's daughter. Yet, he still laments his inability to shudder. While he sleeps, his wife, with a maid's help, pours a bucket of icy water and gudgeons over him. He leaps up in sudden shock, finally exclaiming, "Now I know what it is to shudder!"
