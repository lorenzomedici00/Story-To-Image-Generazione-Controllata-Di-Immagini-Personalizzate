A stern widower proposes to a widow with her daughter observing. He tests her sincerity by filling an old, worn leather boot with water. Miraculously, the boot holds the water perfectly. Convinced, the man and the widow celebrate their simple, joyful wedding. The man's daughter watches from afar.

Initially, the man's beautiful daughter receives promised milk and wine. Soon, the jealous step-mother's expression hardens. She forces her step-daughter to drink water, while her own ugly daughter enjoys rich milk and wine. The step-mother glares, consumed by envy of the step-daughter's growing grace.

In deep winter, the cruel step-mother forces the thinly-clad step-daughter into the frozen, snow-covered forest, seeking strawberries. Shivering, the girl discovers a quaint, welcoming cottage. Inside, three tiny, kindly old men sit by a warm fire. She humbly shares her single piece of hard bread with them.

Grateful, the little men bestow three magical gifts upon the step-daughter. She will grow more beautiful, speak words of gold, and marry a king. She then sweeps snow, finding ripe strawberries. Returning home, she speaks, and glittering gold coins shower from her mouth, astonishing the step-mother and envious step-sister.

The envious step-sister, rude to the little men, receives curses: daily ugliness and leaping toads from her mouth. Later, the step-daughter, cutting ice, is found by a smitten King who makes her his Queen. After the evil duo throws her into a river, she returns as a duck, then is restored by the King's sword. They face doom in a barrel of nails.
