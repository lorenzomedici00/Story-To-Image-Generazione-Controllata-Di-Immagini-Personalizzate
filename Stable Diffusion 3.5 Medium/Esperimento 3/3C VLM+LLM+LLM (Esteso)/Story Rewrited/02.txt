A three-year-old girl, dressed in rags, is gently lifted by the Virgin Mary, descending in a radiant glow with a crown of shining stars. They arrive in a lush, ethereal paradise. The girl, now in splendor, plays joyfully with smiling cherubic angels, eating sugar-cakes and drinking sweet milk.

At fourteen, the radiant Virgin Mary, preparing for a long journey, entrusts the girl with a large ring holding thirteen keys. The Virgin points to a small, unassuming key, strictly forbidding the girl to unlock the thirteenth, hidden door in the heavenly realm.

Alone, her curiosity becomes unbearable. Ignoring the angels' soft warnings, she creeps towards a small, unadorned door. She inserts the tiny key, turning it. The door opens, revealing a blinding, fiery splendor with a majestic, ethereal presence inside.

The girl reaches out, touching the intense light; her finger immediately turns solid gold. Terrified, she slams the door shut, the golden mark stark. When the Virgin Mary returns, the girl lies three times, but her glowing golden finger dramatically exposes her disobedience.

Cast into a desolate wilderness, the mute girl hides in a hollow tree, her long hair forming a cloak. She is found by a King and becomes Queen. Later, tied to a burning stake, she confesses aloud; flames extinguish, her children and voice return, granting forgiveness.
